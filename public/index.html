How to Play Craps

A practical beginner-to-table guide.

Craps in plain English

The dice create the action. The bets decide what you are rooting for.

One player is the shooter and rolls two dice. Most of the game revolves around whether a number is established as the point and whether that point repeats before a 7.

1. The Come-Out Roll and the Point

A new hand begins with a come-out roll. On a standard Pass Line bet, 7 or 11 wins immediately; 2, 3 or 12 loses. A 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 or 10 becomes the point.

Once a point is established, the shooter continues until either the point repeats or a 7 rolls.

Traditional: 2, 3 and 12 are come-out craps numbers. Crapless: 2, 3, 11 and 12 can become points instead of being immediate Pass Line losses/wins in the traditional way.
2. Pass Line and Don't Pass

Pass Line is the basic bet with the shooter: win on 7/11 on the come-out, lose on 2/3/12, then win if the point repeats before 7.

Don't Pass generally takes the opposite side in Traditional Craps. Rules for the 12 can include a push. Many Crapless tables do not offer Don't Pass.

3. Odds — the cleanest payout math

After a point is established, many casinos allow an additional Odds wager. Odds pay true mathematical odds with no built-in house advantage on the Odds portion.

  • 4 / 10: 2:1
  • 5 / 9: 3:2
  • 6 / 8: 6:5

Crapless points 2/12 and 3/11 have different true-odds ratios.

4. Place Bets

A Place bet says a selected number will roll before a 7. The wager normally remains working after a win unless you take it down or the casino procedure says otherwise.

  • 6 / 8 pay 7:6
  • 5 / 9 pay 7:5
  • 4 / 10 pay 9:5

Crapless tables may also allow Place bets on 2, 3, 11 and 12 with different payouts.

5. Buy and Lay Bets

A Buy bet pays true odds on a number and usually carries a commission called the vig. A Lay bet is the opposite: you are betting that 7 will appear before the selected number.

Buy vig timing matters: some casinos collect the vig when the Buy bet is placed. Others book the full Buy bet and collect the vig only if the number wins. On an up-front-vig table, the payout on the hit is the full true-odds win because the commission was already paid. On a win-only table, the commission is taken from the winning payout.

Craps Master stores that rule in each casino profile and changes the drill procedure automatically. Rounding also varies by property. Many tables round the vig down to the dollar with a $1 minimum — for example, a $25 Buy on the 4 pays just $1 — while others charge to the cent or round up. Set the rounding in your profile so the drill matches your table.

6. Come and Don't Come

A Come bet behaves much like a new Pass Line bet made after a point already exists. Its first roll acts like its own come-out roll, and if a number is established the wager travels to that number. Don't Come is the opposite-side version where offered.

7. The Field

The Field is a one-roll bet on 2, 3, 4, 9, 10, 11 or 12. Most winning Field numbers pay even money, while 2 and/or 12 may pay double or triple depending on the casino.

This is a perfect example of why the selected casino profile matters.

8. Hardways and Proposition Bets

Hardways win when 4, 6, 8 or 10 appears as a matching pair before an easy version of that number or a 7. Center-table proposition bets such as Horn, C&E, Any Craps, Any Seven and hops are generally one-roll or special-condition bets with larger payouts.

Dealers often need these payouts instantly, making them especially useful for memorization drills.

9. Number Completion Bonus

Many layouts offer a bonus based on rolling groups of numbers before a 7. Craps Master uses generic terminology: Small Side, All Numbers and Tall Side.

The exact payout and wager limits vary significantly by property, so the profile is especially important here.

10. Traditional vs. Crapless

Traditional Craps uses 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10 as point numbers. Crapless expands the point numbers to include 2, 3, 11 and 12, changing the come-out behavior and creating additional Place/Buy/Odds calculations.

The basic table rhythm remains familiar, but payout memorization becomes more demanding — one reason Crapless has its own first-class mode in Craps Master.

11. Who Does What at the Table?

Shooter: rolls the dice. Stick: controls the dice and commonly handles/announces center action. Base dealers: handle line, Come, Place, Buy, Lay, Field, odds and player action on their side. Box / floor supervision: oversees procedure, bankroll and disputes.

Craps Master uses one training engine because both players and dealers benefit from recognizing the same payout quickly and correctly.

12. Profit vs. Total Return

If a $30 Place 6 wins $35, the profit is $35. If the original wager is returned at the same time, the total returned is $65.

Craps Master normally asks for profit so you learn the actual payoff. Always read the question wording.

Beginner learning path

Pass Line → Odds → Place 6/8 → Place 5/9 → Place 4/10 → Field → Come → Hardways → proposition bets → Buy/Lay → Crapless extreme numbers.
